# BOUCH Voice & Copy

## Overview

BOUCH sounds like a tradesperson's site, not a tech agency. The tone is grounded, no-nonsense, and practical. "Here's what I do, here's the evidence, let's talk." Not inspirational. Not evangelical. Not slick.

When working on copy for bouch.dev, use one of three modes below depending on the task.

---

## Three Modes

### Mode 1: Rewrite

Apply BOUCH voice to existing copy. Steps:

1. First run the **humaniser** skill to strip AI patterns (em dashes, AI vocabulary, rule of three, promotional inflation)
2. Then apply the BOUCH-specific rules below on top
3. Return the rewritten copy with a brief note on what changed

### Mode 2: New Angles

Generate 2-3 distinct copy angles for a given section or topic. Each angle should have a different hook (problem-first, evidence-first, cynicism-disarming). Label them clearly. Keep them in the BOUCH voice — no inspirational framing, no buzzwords.

### Mode 3: Voice Review

Review a piece of copy against the rules below. Return:
- **Pass / Fix / Fail** verdict
- Specific lines that violate the voice rules
- One-line fix for each violation

## BOUCH Voice Rules

### Language to Use

These come directly from how UK business owners describe what they want:

- "figure out what actually works for your business"
- "audit your workflows"
- "specific to your situation"
- "practical steps"
- "without breaking the bank"
- "clean, simple workflow"
- "what's actually moving the needle"
- "help your team actually use it"
- "honest assessment"
- "one workflow at a time"
- "works with what you already have"
- "we'll set it up with you"
- "your team" (not "your organisation")
- "actually use" (not "adopt")
- "save time" (not "drive efficiency")
- "show your team" (not "change management")

### Language to Avoid

These trigger instant distrust with UK SMBs:

- "transform your business with AI"
- "leverage AI" / "leverage" anything
- "AI-powered"
- "revolutionise"
- "cutting-edge" / "disruptive"
- "10x your productivity"
- "scale with AI"
- "automate everything"
- "digital transformation"
- "unlock potential"
- "future-proof"
- "paradigm shift"
- "seamless integration"
- Unsubstantiated promises of hours or money saved
- "the only" / "the first" / superlative ownership claims

### Formatting Rules

- British spelling throughout (organise, analyse, colour, behaviour)
- No em dashes in marketing copy (AI tell)
- Short sentences. Direct. No filler.
- Lead with the answer, not the reasoning
- No exclamation marks unless genuinely warranted

## Audience Context

**Who they are:** UK SMBs, 10-200 employees. Managers and founders tasked with "getting the team to use AI." They have bought tools (Copilot, ChatGPT licences) but nobody uses them. They are sceptical of consultants. They have been burned by slick promises before.

**What they fear:** Wasting more money on something that doesn't stick. Looking stupid in front of their team. Buying another tool nobody uses.

**What they need to hear:** That the problem is normal, that the fix is practical, and that someone who actually builds things is on their side.

**What NOT to say to them:** Anything that sounds like a tech vendor or a management consultant.

---

## Key Messages (for angle generation)

Use these as the foundation for any new angles. They come from positioning:

1. **The bottleneck is human, not technical.** Most teams have the tools. The problem is nobody knows how to use them in their actual workflow.
2. **One workflow at a time.** Not a strategy. Not a roadmap. One thing that saves time, done properly.
3. **Paul builds things.** This isn't theory. Live URLs, real clients, real outcomes.
4. **Practical and individual.** Every person in a team needs something different. One-size training doesn't work.
5. **The audit comes first.** Start by finding where time is actually lost (not where it looks like it's lost).

## Page-Specific Notes

### Homepage hero

Hook with the problem, not the solution. The reader should think "that's me" before they read the second sentence.

Good pattern: [common frustrating situation] + [honest observation] + [what's actually available]

Bad pattern: "AI is changing everything. Here's how to get ahead."

### Services page

Lead with the customer question (e.g. "We bought tools but nobody uses them"), then explain what the service actually is. Avoid service names as headlines — the customer doesn't know what "Mode 2 Training" means.

### Tools / Products page

State what the skill does in one sentence. Then the problem it solves. Keep features as bullet points. No hype.

### Work / Case studies

Problem first, outcome second. Name the client type (e.g. "a property investment firm") if not named. Use real numbers if available. Avoid "helped X achieve Y through our innovative approach."

### About page

Paul's background should read like a CV bullet, not a brand story. Credentials and projects. No "passion for AI" language.

## What Good Looks Like

**Before (AI-generated, generic):**
> "At BOUCH, we leverage cutting-edge AI solutions to transform your business workflows, driving efficiency and unlocking the potential of your team."

**After (BOUCH voice):**
> "Most teams have the tools. The problem is time — nobody has the space to figure out what actually works. That's what the audit is for."

---

**Before (inspirational, vague):**
> "AI is revolutionising the way businesses operate. Don't get left behind."

**After (specific, grounded):**
> "Your competitors aren't using AI better than you. Most of them bought the same tools and hit the same wall. The difference is in the workflow, not the software."
